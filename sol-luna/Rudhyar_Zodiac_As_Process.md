# Rudhyar's Zodiac as Process

Dane Rudhyar gives the zodiac back to time. A sign becomes a phase of the living year, a mode of solar force, a step in the passage from germination to collective seed. The twelve signs form one organism of meaning. Each degree is one syllable in the same poem.

## Synopsis

Rudhyar's zodiac has four linked forms.

First, the zodiac is the yearly cycle of light and darkness. Aries, Cancer, Libra, and Capricorn are the four hinges of the year: equinox, solstice, equinox, solstice. The signs unfold from the relation between two powers Rudhyar calls the Day-force and the Night-force.

Second, every sign is a phase. Aries has meaning because it begins the spring up-rush; Pisces has meaning because it dissolves the completed cycle into the next seed. The sign is read by function, position, and direction within the whole.

Third, the 360 degrees are a mandala. The Sabian Symbols become a complete cycle of symbolic phases, a time-mandala whose images serve meaning when each image is held in sequence.

Fourth, the zodiac is distinct from the houses. The zodiac belongs to celestial and seasonal order; the houses belong to the person's terrestrial field of experience. Planets are functions. Houses are fields. Signs are modes of energy and phases of the solar process.

## The zodiac begins as the year

Rudhyar's first move is simple: the zodiac belongs to the experience of the year before it belongs to a chart wheel. In *The Pulse of Life*, he strips the signs of their later fortune-telling shell and returns them to the relationship of light and darkness.

> "The Zodiac which is used in our astrology has very little, if anything at all, to do with distant stars as entities in themselves. It is an ancient record of the cyclic series of transformations actually experienced by man throughout the year; a record written in symbolic language using the stars as a merely convenient, graphic way of building up symbolic images appealing to the imagination of a humanity childlike enough to be more impressed by pictures than by abstract and generalized processes of thought."\
> — Dane Rudhyar, *The Pulse of Life*, "The Nature of the Zodiac"

The year is a breathing body. Rudhyar names the two breaths Day-force and Night-force. The spring equinox brings equality with the Day-force rising. The summer solstice brings the Day-force to its maximum. The autumn equinox restores equality with the Night-force rising. The winter solstice brings the Night-force to its maximum. Aries, Cancer, Libra, and Capricorn are the cardinal turns of that body.

> "Whenever there are two forces alternately waxing and waning in relative strength, four critical, basic moments must of necessity be found. Thus:
> 1. At the winter solstice (Christmas) the Day-force is at its weakest and the Night-force at its strongest level. This is the beginning of the zodiacal Sign: Capricorn.
> 2. At the spring equinox (around March 21) the Day-force which has increased in strength while the Night-force decreased, equals in power that Night-force. Zodiacal Sign: Aries.
> 3. At the summer solstice (around June 21) the Day-force reaches a maximum energy, the Night-force its lowest ebb. Zodiacal Sign: Cancer.
> 4. At the fall equinox (around September 21) the two forces are again equal, the Night-force having grown stronger ever since the beginning of the summer. Zodiacal Sign: Libra."\
> — Dane Rudhyar, *The Pulse of Life*, "The Day-force and the Night-force"

This makes the zodiac an order of proportion. Day and Night both act in every sign. Their ratio changes. A sign carries a quality because the whole year stands there in a particular balance.

> "What the study of the Zodiac will teach us is, first of all, that, while there are always two forces in operation in every situation and in every experience, understanding and decision are never a matter of 'either-or,' but of 'more or less.' There is dualism; but the dualism of a dynamic process in which both opposites constantly interpenetrate and transform each other. Because of this, no entity and no experience is either good or bad, constructive or destructive, light or dark. Everything is in everything. What changes is the proportion in which the combination occurs."\
> — Dane Rudhyar, *The Pulse of Life*, "Two Approaches to Life"

The sign is a ratio of the whole, not an isolated label.

## A sign is a phase

Rudhyar's language sharpens in *A New Approach to the Zodiac*. He names the error directly: fixed categories make the signs look like separate entities. The modern correction is process.

> "Existence is seen more and more as a process — thus, as a series of more or less exactly 'cyclic' transformations. We realize that what is most important is the process-as-a-whole, not the separate events to which we gave set characteristics. To consider what happens at a particular time as an 'entity' with a name inevitably emphasizes its separation from other entities; whereas if we think of any event and phenomenon — or even of any person, race or society — as a phase of some vast process, we gain a new perspective on life — a truly 'modern' outlook."\
> — Dane Rudhyar, *A New Approach to the Zodiac*, Part One

The zodiac receives the same treatment.

> "From this same 'modern' point of view, the zodiac represents also a process. It should be referred to the basic human experience of the seasons and applied to all that is affected by the seasonal rhythm of the year. Indeed, it seems very likely that the zodiac was originally a seasonal cycle, a time factor."\
> — Dane Rudhyar, *A New Approach to the Zodiac*, Part One

The four cardinal signs give the living proof. They are experienced before they are named.

> "Aries, Cancer, Libra, Capricorn considered as signs of the zodiac are names given by our civilization to certain basic turning points or phases in the cycle of seasonal changes which affects the rhythm of all that lives on the earth surface. These changes are factual, existential, emotional — thus, real. They do not depend on celestial maps drawn by priests or intellectuals, stargazers or astronomers. You and I live them."\
> — Dane Rudhyar, *A New Approach to the Zodiac*, Part One, "The Experience of Seasonal Change"

Rudhyar then expands the zodiac into three interwoven levels. Life begins at Aries. Personality begins at Leo. Mentality begins at Sagittarius. Each begins with fire; each proceeds through its own fourfold rhythm.

> "One can, therefore, speak of three zodiacs, each starting at the time of the year most conducive to the type of activity with which it correlates. The element fire represents always the start of the fourfold process at each of the three levels. These refer to life, personality and mentality."\
> — Dane Rudhyar, *A New Approach to the Zodiac*, Part Two

> "These three processes constantly interact, and each of the phases of one process is anticipated or reflected by intermediary phases in the other processes. Existence is indeed one vast, complex process. Every human being participates in it according to the particular possibilities represented by his or her birth-chart and according to the timing and place of his or her activities at this or that level of existence."\
> — Dane Rudhyar, *A New Approach to the Zodiac*, Part Two

This is why Rudhyar's signs resist stereotype. Aries is the life-fire of renewal. Leo is personality-fire. Sagittarius is mental fire. Each carries fire, yet each begins a different process.

## Aries begins the up-rush

Rudhyar's Aries is the seed breaking the crust. It is adolescence, emergence, and the right to begin.

> "Aries — Piercing through the crust of the soil which the melting of snow softened, the sprouting seed forces its life into the light of the sun. The fervent up-reaching of spring brings forth the wonder of germination. The Day-force now balances in intensity the waning Night-force... The new star asserts his right before the foot-lights of the human consciousness. Henceforth, the show will be his. Yet, his voice is unassured; his countenance reveals hidden fears in its very bravado. In Aries the human personality experiences its phase of adolescence."\
> — Dane Rudhyar, *The Pulse of Life*, "ARIES"

> "The Aries type is constantly in the process of forming himself. He has no sense of set selfhood; no sense of set boundaries. He is ever open to the inrush of universal, non-personified Life. He is never a finished product, and he cares little for finishing what he attempts. He is taken up by the act of creating, not by his creations."\
> — Dane Rudhyar, *The Pulse of Life*, "ARIES"

The whole sequence follows this rule. Taurus substantiates. Gemini differentiates and links. Cancer encloses. Leo radiates. Virgo discriminates. Libra meets the other. Scorpio intensifies the shared force. Sagittarius abstracts and aims. Capricorn condenses. Aquarius universalizes. Pisces dissolves and offers the residue to the next beginning.

The sign speaks by position.

## The 360 degrees are a mandala

*An Astrological Mandala* carries the same doctrine down to the degree. Rudhyar reads the Sabian Symbols as a cycle of 360 phases, not as a bag of picturesque omens.

> "The symbols can be applied to any cycle of experience that can be conveniently divided into 360 phases. For instance, it could be used to interpret the cycle of precession of the equinoxes, if one were absolutely sure when this cycle began. It can be used with reference to the cycle of daily motion of the Mid Heaven and Ascendant. Nevertheless, the cycle of the year—the tropical zodiac—is the most natural field for its application."\
> — Dane Rudhyar, *An Astrological Mandala*, Introduction, p. 5

A mandala has form and rhythm. The zodiac is one such mandala.

> "The entire zodiac constitutes a mandala. There are time mandalas as well as space mandalas. The cycle of transformations studied in this book is a time mandala. It has rhythm as well as form. Any language, particularly any poem, also has rhythm and form. The 360 Sabian symbols are words in a vast cosmic poem whose meaning transcends the often banal images visualized by the clairvoyant."\
> — Dane Rudhyar, *An Astrological Mandala*, Part One, ch. 1, pp. 11-12

The degree belongs to the cycle the way a word belongs to a sentence. Its private image matters after its position is known.

> "When we deal with any cyclic series of factors or phases, merely to consider the character, quality and value of the symbolic representation of any one of these factors as a separate entity without an essential (or structural) relationship to all the others makes no sense to the holistic mind. Every phase no doubt has a character of its own which can be described in one way or another, but this character should be given some kind of functional or 'organic' meaning in terms of the cyclic process as a whole."\
> — Dane Rudhyar, *An Astrological Mandala*, Part One, ch. 1, p. 17

Rudhyar distinguishes the archetypal circle from the lived year. The 360-degree zodiac is a formula of meaning. The days of the year are the facts through which that formula is lived.

> "The 360-degree zodiac is a formula of archetypal relationships; but our human experience presents to our consciousness a slightly larger sequence of days and nights. The 360-degree cycle refers to the meaning of experience; the days-and-nights sequence to the facts of experience."\
> — Dane Rudhyar, *An Astrological Mandala*, Part One, ch. 1, p. 16

This distinction preserves the symbolic circle without reducing the living year to arithmetic. The year gives the fact. The circle gives the intelligible form.

## Matrix, signs, planets, houses

In *The Zodiac as the Universal Matrix*, Rudhyar adds a more spatial language. The zodiac can be read as the yearly cycle of transformations; it can also be read as a formative field surrounding Earth.

> "From this approach the zodiac can be seen as a yearly 'cycle of transformations,' the meaning of natural yearly transformations being extended to apply to the psychological-mental and social levels of human development. The zodiac, from such a point of view, is a factor of time. It is a cycle — a process."\
> — Dane Rudhyar, *The Zodiac as the Universal Matrix*, Introduction

> "The zodiac is a 'universal matrix' — thus a place; yet a matrix is not an ordinary kind of space. It is an electromagnetic 'field' upon which formative Powers are focused. It is living substance in the process of being built into an organism. It is not a 'house' or a collection of neatly defined spaces, but the crucible of life."\
> — Dane Rudhyar, quoted in Michael R. Meyer's Foreword to *The Zodiac as the Universal Matrix*

> "We can think of a sign of the zodiac as a region in which one aspect of the solar force is concentrated in work; but if we picture this place in static terms we are greatly mistaken. The essence of the zodiac is dynamic activity at the level of the formation of substance, of polar electromagnetic energies, of life-processes."\
> — Dane Rudhyar, quoted in Michael R. Meyer's Foreword to *The Zodiac as the Universal Matrix*

This matrix language clarifies the difference between signs, planets, and houses. Signs name the mode of solar life-process. Planets focus functions through those modes. Houses locate those functions inside the person's field of experience.

Rudhyar gives the house distinction in *The Astrological Houses*:

> "When the modern astrologer speaks of these two frames of reference for the measuring of the motion of Sun, Moon, and planets he at once mentions that the first refers to the apparent yearly motion of the Sun around the zodiac... and the second to the daily rotation of our globe around its polar axis... But to return to the two frames of reference used for the measure of the positions, the angular relationships, and the cycles of Sun, Moon, and planets: the first one is what we today call the zodiac; the second, the circle of houses."\
> — Dane Rudhyar, *The Astrological Houses*, Part One, "Zodiacs and Houses"

The zodiac is the sky's cycle of formative qualities. The houses are the terrestrial circle of experience centered on the breathing person.

## What Rudhyar gives The Book of Sol

The Book of Sol already treats the zodiac as a solar grammar. Rudhyar supplies the modern symbolic language for that grammar.

The signs are solar phases. The degrees are a time-mandala. The cardinal signs are turns in the year-breath. Day-force and Night-force give every sign its inner proportion. The planets do not command from outside the person; they focus functions inside a whole. The chart is a symbolic structure of meaning, not a sentence imposed by external powers.

> "Astrology can be seen, in the light of this world-philosophy, as a remarkable tool for the understanding of human experience considered as the field for a cyclic interplay of polar energies or attitudes. Astrology is a means to see human experience as an organic whole, a technique of interpretation, an 'algebra of life.'"\
> — Dane Rudhyar, *The Pulse of Life*, "Two Approaches to Life"

The zodiac is the annual poem of Sol. Rudhyar teaches how to read it without breaking the poem into twelve dead words.
