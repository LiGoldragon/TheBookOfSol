# Carlos Castañeda — Power, Intent, Warrior, and Freedom: Bilingual Source Notes

This is a compact bibliographic and terminological note for later original Book of
Sol prose. It treats the Castañeda cycle as a twentieth-century literary
teaching-system. It does not establish Yaqui, Toltec, or Indigenous doctrine.

## Concise bilingual thematic summaries

### Poder / Power

In the cycle, *poder* gathers capacity, attention, and a relation to an
unfamiliar world; it carries no stable modern equivalent. Later prose can use
"power" as a literary word when it names Castañeda's narrative and should write
its own account of responsibility, attention, or discipline in its own voice.

En el ciclo, *poder* reúne capacidad, atención y una relación con un mundo
desconocido; no posee un equivalente moderno fijo. Una composición posterior
puede usar "power" al nombrar la narrativa de Castañeda y debe formular por sí
misma cualquier argumento sobre responsabilidad, atención o disciplina.

### Intento / Intent

*Intent* is more precise than the supplied "willpower" for the later Castañeda
rubric. The content heading "Stalking Intent and the Dreaming Position" supports
that distinction. A writer may contrast intention with force of will, provided
the contrast is identified as fresh interpretation rather than translation.

*Intento* es más preciso que "willpower" para la rúbrica tardía de Castañeda. El
título "Stalking Intent and the Dreaming Position" respalda esa distinción. Un
autor puede contrastar intención y fuerza de voluntad si lo identifica como una
interpretación nueva, no como traducción establecida.

### Guerrero / Warrior

The warrior frames a discipline of attention under mortality. The existing Book
of Sol essay [The Warrior](../../political/The_Warrior.md) uses the figure but
does not yet supply edition or page references. Any revision should ground short
quotations in an identified edition and keep the surrounding prose original.

El guerrero encuadra una disciplina de atención bajo la mortalidad. El ensayo
existente de Book of Sol, [The Warrior](../../political/The_Warrior.md), emplea
la figura sin referencias de edición o página. Toda revisión debe fundamentar
las citas breves en una edición identificada y mantener original la prosa que las
rodea.

### Libertad / Freedom

Freedom is a horizon in the teaching-system, often bound to awareness, death,
and departure. It belongs to the narrative's metaphysical register. Its value for
new prose lies in the pressure it places on a life, not in a factual promise that
the books establish an afterlife.

La libertad es un horizonte del sistema de enseñanza, ligado con frecuencia a
la conciencia, la muerte y la partida. Pertenece al registro metafísico de la
narrativa. Su valor para nueva prosa reside en la presión que ejerce sobre una
vida, no en una promesa factual de vida posterior.

## Supplied headings whose source status remains unresolved

The following are **not confirmed published Castañeda or Armando Torres titles**
in the bibliographic records checked for this collection:

- `La meta`
- `La mitad del brujo`
- `La mitad del guerrero`
- `La mitad del vidente`
- `La mitad de la libertad`

They can form an original bilingual sequence if marked as such. Literal working
English forms are **The Aim**, **The Sorcerer's Half**, **The Warrior's Half**,
**The Seer's Half**, and **The Half of Freedom**. These are editorial proposals,
not published translations. `El guerrero / The Warrior` is a sound thematic pair
and the title of an existing Book of Sol essay, while still requiring a separate
edition-specific source trail for any attributed prose.

## Short quotation record

The 2009 English catalogue record for Castañeda's own quotation collection,
*The Wheel of Time*, displays the short line: "a warrior laughs and laughs."
[Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Wheel_Of_Time.html?id=aqz4lHgxNNYC)
It is under 25 words. Verify the edition and page before publishing it as an
epigraph.

UC Press describes *The Teachings of Don Juan* as available to readers
"Whether read as ethnographic fact or creative fiction."
[UC Press](https://www.ucpress.edu/books/the-teachings-of-don-juan/hardcover)
That is a publisher description, not Castañeda's own claim.

## Reception boundary

Jensen's account in *HAU: Journal of Ethnographic Theory* records longstanding
scholarly doubts about the ethnographic status of the work and the lasting damage
to Castañeda's anthropological reputation.
[Jensen 2013](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.14318/hau3.3.013)
The collection therefore attributes teachings to the narrative or to a named
author rather than to verified Yaqui or Toltec traditions.
